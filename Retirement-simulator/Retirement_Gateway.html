How retirement really works

Every decision changes the next one.

Most retirement plans begin and end with a savings target. But the same portfolio can create very different lives depending on when income begins, which accounts are used, where you live, and how healthcare is funded.

⏱When you retire

Changes the length of your bridge years, healthcare options, income needs, and conversion window.

🧾How you create income

Changes taxes, ACA eligibility, Medicare surcharges, required distributions, and legacy value.

🌎Where you live

Changes housing, taxes, medical access, daily life, and proximity to people you love.

About the Retirement Blueprint

The Retirement Blueprint is a guided, step-by-step planning journey. It runs in your browser and saves your answers on your own device, so you can stop and return later.

What the Blueprint does

It walks through the decisions that shape a retirement in a sensible order: your starting point, an orientation forecast, the kind of retirement life you want, where you might live, how you will cover healthcare, and finally a coordinated tax and withdrawal strategy. Each step uses what you entered before, so later steps are tailored to your earlier choices.

How to use it

  1. Press Design My Retirement and answer the starting-point questions. Round numbers are fine.
  2. Review your orientation forecast to see the general direction of your plan.
  3. Explore lifestyle options and test which ones fit your priorities and constraints.
  4. Choose location and healthcare paths.
  5. Finish in the financial strategy lab to compare Roth conversion and withdrawal strategies, then stress-test them in the Scenario Lab.

Assumptions and privacy

  • Projections use simplified tax rules and steady return and inflation assumptions that you can change in later steps.
  • Your answers are stored in your browser's local storage, not sent to WealthLanding. Clearing your browser data removes them.
  • The Blueprint is an educational planning aid, not a financial plan or advice.

Example: Bill and Tom

The page introduces Bill and Tom, two illustrative married retirees who both stop working at 60 with the same $3.8 million: $2.8 million in traditional accounts, $700,000 in a brokerage account and $300,000 in Roth accounts. Tom withdraws as needed and takes required minimum distributions from 73. Bill converts part of his traditional balance to Roth each year from 60 to 72 while staying inside the 22% federal bracket. With the same returns and spending, Bill's heirs receive about $6.6 million after income tax at 85 and Tom's about $5.8 million, mostly because Tom's heirs would owe tax on his remaining pretax balance. The figures come from one shared calculation, also used in the case study Bill Left $6.6 Million. Tom Left $5.8 Million., which lists every assumption. Different assumptions give different results.
