🧾 Max the Tax Code

In your peak earning years, taxes are your biggest lifetime expense — bigger than housing. Every dollar you shield from taxes is a dollar that compounds for decades. Tax planning in your 30s is worth more than stock picking.

Why this matters

Most people spend hours researching investments to squeeze out an extra 0.5% of return — and zero minutes on tax strategy that could save them 20–30% on every dollar invested. The tax code hands you legal shelters; this step is about using all of them, in the right order.

The framework

  • HSA first (if eligible): the only triple-tax-advantaged account. Deductible contributions, tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawals for medical. At 65 it becomes a traditional IRA. Pay medical bills from cash and let the HSA compound.
  • Roth vs. traditional: simple rule — if your current marginal tax rate is LOWER than what you expect in retirement, favor Roth; if HIGHER, favor traditional/pre-tax. Most 30s earners in high-tax states benefit from pre-tax 401(k) + Roth IRA combo.
  • 401(k) to the annual IRS max once Steps 1–3 are funded. The limit rises most years — check the current figure.
  • Mega backdoor Roth if your plan allows after-tax contributions with in-service rollovers — a hidden superpower.

Do this this week

  • Confirm HSA eligibility and max it before Roth IRA
  • Split 401(k) contributions Roth vs. pre-tax using the rule above
  • Ask HR if your plan allows mega backdoor Roth
  • Harvest any taxable-account losses before year-end

⚠️ Common mistakes

  • Ignoring the HSA because "it's just for medical bills."
  • Going 100% Roth at a 32%+ marginal rate.
  • Leaving an old HSA in cash instead of investing it.
