📊 Am I on track?

Am I behind? How much should I have saved by 30 / 35 / 40?

Fidelity's rule of thumb: 1× your salary saved by 30, 3× by 40. But that's retirement accounts only — add home equity, subtract debt, and don't treat it as a grade. If you're behind, raising your savings rate now matters far more than the number.

Go deeper

What percentage of my income should I be saving?

15% of gross income is the minimum target; 20%+ if you're catching up. Can't do 15% today? Start at your full 401(k) match and add 1% every quarter — you'll barely feel it.

Go deeper

I'm 35 and still living paycheck to paycheck on a good salary — what am I doing wrong?

It's almost never an income problem — it's a systems problem: no automatic savings, and lifestyle rising with every raise. Fix it with automation on payday, then split every future raise 50/50 between saving and spending.

📈 Investing

Roth or traditional 401(k) — which one do I pick?

If your tax rate now is lower than you expect in retirement, favor Roth; if higher, favor traditional. Most 30s earners do well with a pre-tax 401(k) plus a Roth IRA.

Go deeper

What order do I fund things — 401(k), IRA, HSA?

Follow the waterfall: 401(k) to the match → HSA → Roth IRA → max the 401(k) → taxable brokerage. Each step unlocks only after the previous one is full.

Go deeper

Should I pay off debt or invest?

Match first, always. Then kill debt above ~7% before investing more; below ~5%, invest while paying on schedule; split the 5–7% middle. The interest rate is the referee.

Go deeper

What do I actually invest in?

Low-cost total-market index funds. You don't need to pick stocks or time anything — your contribution rate matters far more than your fund choice.

Go deeper

I have cash sitting around — lump sum in or dollar-cost average?

Investing it all at once has won roughly two-thirds of the time historically. DCA over a few months is fine if it keeps you invested — sitting in cash waiting for “the right time” is the real mistake.

Go deeper

Should I roll over my old 401(k) from a past job?

Yes — into your new 401(k) or a rollover IRA via direct trustee-to-trustee transfer. Never cash out: income tax plus a 10% penalty if you're under 59½.

🏠 Housing

Should I rent or buy? Is renting throwing money away?

Neither is throwing money away — it's math. If 5% of the home's price per year (taxes, maintenance, cost of capital) exceeds rent on a comparable place, renting and investing the difference usually wins. Run your own numbers.

Go deeper

How much house can I actually afford?

The 28/36 rule: housing costs at or below 28% of gross monthly income, all debt payments at or below 36%. And buy below what the bank approves — that's their limit, not yours.

Go deeper

Should I wait for interest rates to drop before buying?

You can refinance a rate, but you can't refinance a purchase price. Buy when the payment fits and you'll stay 5+ years; refinance later if rates fall.

Go deeper

Should I pause retirement saving to save for a down payment faster?

Never drop below the full 401(k) match — that's free money you'd never get back. Pausing only the above-match contributions for a defined 12–18 month sprint is the compromise.

Go deeper

20% down or buy sooner with PMI?

20% avoids PMI and proves affordability — but don't drain your emergency fund to get there. Buying sooner with PMI can make sense if prices are rising fast and you'll reach 20% equity within a few years.

🛡️ Family & protection

How much life insurance do I need — and term or whole?

10× gross income in 20–30 year term life; skip whole life. A healthy 35-year-old pays roughly $25–$40/month per $1M of coverage.

Go deeper

Do I really need a will? What happens if I die without one?

Yes. Without one, the state decides who gets your assets and who raises your kids. A basic will plus named beneficiaries on every account covers most people.

Go deeper

When do I start a 529, and how much goes in?

After your own retirement is on track — you can borrow for college, not for retirement. Then: capture any state tax deduction, automate monthly contributions, and use an age-based portfolio.

Go deeper

How do we handle money as a couple — combine or separate?

Combined, separate, or hybrid (joint bills + personal accounts) all work. What matters is a monthly 20-minute money date — surprises cause fights, not systems.

Go deeper

🧾 Taxes

How do I legally pay less in taxes?

Max pre-tax accounts (401(k), HSA) in high-earning years, use Roth when your rate is low, and harvest losses in taxable accounts. Taxes are your biggest lifetime expense — planning beats stock-picking.

Go deeper

What is an HSA and should I actually use one?

If you have a high-deductible health plan: yes. Triple tax-advantaged — deductible in, tax-free growth, tax-free out for medical. Let it compound; pay medical bills from cash.

Go deeper

What's a backdoor / mega backdoor Roth — and do I qualify?

Backdoor Roth: nondeductible IRA contribution → convert; no income limit on conversions. Mega backdoor needs a 401(k) allowing after-tax contributions plus in-service rollovers — ask HR.

Go deeper

💼 Work & lifestyle

How do I stop lifestyle inflation as my pay rises?

The raise splitter: save half of every raise, spend half. Your lifestyle still rises — your savings rate just rises faster.

Go deeper

New car or keep the old one — cash or finance?

The 20/4/10 rule: 20% down, a loan of 4 years max, total car costs under 10% of gross income. A $600/mo payment invested at 8% for 10 years is roughly $110,000 you'll never see.

Go deeper

Higher salary vs. better benefits and 401(k) — how do I compare offers?

Compare total comp: add the 401(k) match, HSA seed money, insurance premiums, and PTO value to the salary. A 6% match on $120k is $7,200 a year — the “lower” offer with great benefits often wins.

Go deeper

How do I know if I'm underpaid?

Benchmark your role and market on levels.fyi, Glassdoor, or BLS data. 10–15% below market is a conversation with your manager; 20%+ is a job search. Closing that gap is usually the highest-ROI financial move available.
