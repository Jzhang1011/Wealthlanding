The framework

The Three Layers of Control

Most retirement anxiety comes from mixing up three kinds of things: what you cannot control, what you can influence, and what you decide directly. Sorting them is the first step to a calmer plan.

1

Outside Your Control

  • Daily market moves
  • Future tax legislation
  • Inflation
  • Policy changes
  • Your exact lifespan

Stop spending your energy trying to predict what you cannot control.

2

Within Your Influence

  • How long you work
  • How much you earn
  • Your health habits
  • When you claim Social Security

Your choices can influence outcomes, even when they cannot guarantee them.

3

Within Your Direct Control

  • Your savings rate
  • Your spending
  • Your account choices
  • Your investment allocation
  • Your withdrawal planning

Focus on the decisions you can make today.

Controlling a decision is not the same as guaranteeing its result. You can choose to save 15% of your income; you cannot choose what the market returns next year. You can choose when to claim Social Security; you cannot choose how long you will collect it. A good plan puts most of its effort into Layer 3, makes thoughtful choices in Layer 2, and builds enough flexibility to absorb whatever Layer 1 brings.

The series

The Seven Principles of Retirement Confidence

Each principle is a decision most people face between their mid-40s and their first years of retirement. Each article stands on its own, with worked examples, a checklist and sources, so you can start wherever your questions are.

  1. Principle 01 · The Longevity Marathon: Will Your Retirement Money Last 30 Years? Understand why retirement may last longer than expected and how inflation and investment growth affect your long-term financial security.
  2. Principle 02 · The Savings Target and the Two-Bucket Approach: How Much Should You Have Saved by 50? Understand retirement savings benchmarks, what to do if you are behind, and how to separate near-term spending needs from long-term growth.
  3. Principle 03 · The Savings Priority Pyramid: Where Should Your Next Dollar of Savings Go? Explore a practical framework for prioritizing emergency savings, employer matches, debt, tax-advantaged accounts, and investments.
  4. Principle 04 · Social Security Timing: Should You Claim Social Security at 62, 67, or 70? Explore the trade-offs between claiming early and delaying benefits, including longevity, household income, and spousal considerations.
  5. Principle 05 · The Medicare Gap: What Medicare Doesn't Cover in Retirement Understand healthcare premiums, out-of-pocket expenses, supplemental coverage, and long-term care planning.
  6. Principle 06 · Tax Box Allocation: Traditional vs. Roth: How to Build a Tax-Smart Retirement Portfolio Learn how tax-deferred, tax-free, and taxable accounts differ and why managing taxable income can matter in retirement.
  7. Principle 07 · The Withdrawal Sequence: Which Retirement Account Should You Spend First? Explore how taxable, traditional retirement, and Roth accounts may fit into a tax-aware withdrawal strategy.

Not sure where to begin?

Where Should You Start?

Pick the worry that sounds most like yours.

  • I'm worried I haven't saved enough: Principle 02: How Much Should You Have Saved by 50?
  • I'm worried about running out of money: Principle 01: Will Your Retirement Money Last 30 Years?
  • I want to make my retirement money more tax-efficient: Principle 06: Traditional vs. Roth: How to Build a Tax-Smart Retirement Portfolio and Principle 07: Which Retirement Account Should You Spend First?

Focus Your Energy on What You Can Control.

You cannot predict every market downturn, future policy change, or healthcare expense. But you can build a thoughtful plan for saving, investing, claiming benefits, managing taxes, and spending your money. Start with one decision. Then make the next one.
