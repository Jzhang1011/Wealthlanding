Downsizing or Relocating

The question is not only “smaller house?” It is whether the next place improves cash flow, taxes, healthcare access, and the retirement plan — after selling costs.

Exclusion$250k / $500k if you qualifyNet outSelling costs before you celebrateTry before buyRent first in a new area

Short answer

Price your current home net of commissions, repairs, staging, moving, and taxes on any gain you cannot exclude. If you sell a main home and meet IRS ownership and use tests, you may exclude up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 married filing jointly). Compare the next housing cost, state and local taxes, and healthcare access. Renting first in a new area reduces the odds of an expensive misfire.

Decision framework for 45–65

Four paths: stay and adapt, downsize nearby, move to another U.S. market, or move abroad for part or all of the year. Each changes housing cost, taxes, insurance, travel, and healthcare. WealthLanding’s Plan by age stages and overseas retirement hub cover adjacent pieces; this guide focuses on the housing balance sheet and fit.

Net equity, not list price

Selling costs and prep

Transaction fees, concessions, repairs, and carrying costs while listed can consume a large slice of a paper gain. Build a net worksheet before you commit to the next purchase.

Main-home exclusion

IRS Topic 701 and Publication 523 explain the $250,000 / $500,000 exclusion, the 2-of-5-year ownership and use tests, the generally once-every-two-years limit, and partial exclusion possibilities. Confirm eligibility before you spend the tax savings.

Buying the next place

Use the affordability and buying guides. A downsize that reloads a large mortgage can erase the cash-flow win.

Rent bridge

Renting for a season in the new city tests climate, healthcare, and daily costs before you buy again.

Fit checks beyond the spreadsheet

Healthcare access

Proximity to clinicians, hospitals, and specialists often matters more at 60 than at 40.

Accessibility

Stairs, bathrooms, driveways, and maintenance load change with age. One-level living is a planning input.

State and local taxes

Compare income, property, and sales taxes together — not income tax alone. Rules are jurisdiction-specific.

Family and community

Grandchildren, caregivers, and social ties have economic value when they reduce paid care or travel friction.

Abroad or snowbird

Medicare generally does not cover most care outside the U.S. Read CMS guidance before dropping coverage. Use the overseas retirement hub for destination research.

Retirement paycheck

Freed equity only helps if it is invested or spent on purpose inside your withdrawal plan — not absorbed by a larger lifestyle.

When the question is daily support, not square footage

Downsizing assumes you still want an independent home. If bathing, dressing, medications or safety needs are outpacing what family and the house can provide, shift to a care-needs frame: home with services, assisted living, or skilled nursing. Age alone is not the signal — daily support needs are. See the daily-support housing guide and official NIA and Medicare pages linked there.

Practical checklist

  • Estimate net sale proceeds after costs and possible taxes on non-excluded gain.
  • Verify §121 ownership/use eligibility with IRS Topic 701 / Publication 523.
  • Price the next home or rental with taxes, insurance, and healthcare travel included.
  • Stress-test cash flow with one spouse retired and with long-term care premiums.
  • Rent in a new area before buying when relocating.
  • Update estate documents and beneficiaries after a sale or move.

Watch out for

  • <strong>Spending paper equity before closing and tax reality.</strong>
  • <strong>Assuming the full $500,000 exclusion without meeting the tests.</strong>
  • <strong>Replacing one oversized house with another expensive “downsize.”</strong>
  • <strong>Moving for tax reasons without pricing healthcare and travel home.</strong>
  • <strong>Dropping Medicare Part B for an abroad move</strong> without understanding re-enrollment rules — see CMS and Plan by age resources.

Primary sources

  • IRS Topic No. 701, Sale of your home — $250,000 / $500,000 exclusion, ownership and use tests
  • IRS Publication 523, Selling Your Home — Eligibility, basis, reporting, partial exclusion
  • IRS Topic No. 409, Capital gains and losses — Long-term vs short-term rates; personal-use loss not deductible
  • IRS Topic No. 559, Net investment income tax — 3.8% NIIT; excluded principal-residence gain generally not NIIT
  • FHFA House Price Index — Repeat-sales index; prices rise and fall by place and time
  • CFPB: Owning a home tools — Mortgage shopping workflow

Rules and figures can change. Confirm on the linked pages before you act.
