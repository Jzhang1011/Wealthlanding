Methodology

How we build guides and tools

Transparent structure, illustrative models, and clear limits — so you can judge the work for yourself.

Life-stage curriculum

Content is organized around common money milestones — early career, building years, peak earning/coordination years, and pre-retirement — rather than a single one-size-fits-all checklist. Each hub links to modules and tools that match the decisions people typically face in that chapter.

We prioritize sequencing (cash flow and risk basics before complex optimization) and plain language. Start in Learn.

Illustrative case studies

Stories and scenarios on the blog and in labs are illustrative. Names, ages, balances, and locations may be composite or hypothetical. They show how trade-offs work under stated assumptions — not a recommendation that your household should copy a path. See Blog & Insights.

Calculators as models

Our calculators and retirement labs are educational models. They help you see sensitivity — what happens if returns, spending, or timing change — not to produce a personalized financial plan.

  • Inputs are user-controlled; defaults are labeled as starting points.
  • Outputs depend on simplified formulas (e.g., constant growth rates, stylized tax brackets, or fixed withdrawal rules).
  • Where a page simplifies reality, we aim to say so near the tool.

Explore Tools & Calculators and the Retirement Lab.

Sources and assumptions (high level)

Where we cite public figures — contribution limits, Social Security concepts, cost-of-living ranges, or historical return ranges — we treat them as educational context that can change. Tool pages typically document key assumptions such as:

  • Contribution rates, expected-return ranges, or inflation placeholders
  • Tax or benefit rules shown in simplified form
  • Lifestyle or city cost inputs used for comparison, not quotes

We do not claim that any modeled path is suitable for your situation. Verify numbers against primary sources and licensed professionals when decisions matter.

Update cadence

We revise hubs, articles, and tools as the site expands and when we catch errors or outdated references. Tax rules, benefit formulas, and market context change; treat page dates and on-page notes as the best available snapshot, and re-check before acting. Corrections are welcome via the footer newsletter form.

Limitations

  • Models omit many real-world frictions: fees, taxes at the household level, sequence-of-returns risk detail, health events, and behavioral gaps.
  • Education on WealthLanding is not personalized advice and does not create an advisor–client relationship.
  • Past or hypothetical returns in examples are not guarantees of future results.

FAQ

Is WealthLanding giving me financial advice?

No. Content and tools are for general education. They are not personalized investment, tax, or legal advice. Decisions remain yours, ideally with a licensed professional when needed.

Are calculator results a plan I can follow?

No. Results are illustrative under the inputs and formulas shown. Change assumptions and the output changes. Use them to learn trade-offs, not as a prescription.

Where do your numbers come from?

Defaults and examples use transparent, high-level assumptions documented on or near each tool. Public rules and ranges can change; always verify against primary sources before acting.

How often is content updated?

We update as we expand the curriculum and when we find errors or outdated references. There is no fixed calendar promise; treat each page as a living educational resource.

Can I suggest a correction?

Yes. Use the newsletter form in the site footer and mention the page URL and what looks wrong. We appreciate careful feedback on formulas and assumptions.
