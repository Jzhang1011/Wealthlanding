Snapshot: what AAdvantage is and how you earn status in 2026

AAdvantage is American's program, and its status system is the simplest of the big three: you earn Loyalty Points and nothing else counts. There is no flight-count requirement and no required number of flown segments. One Loyalty Point is earned for each eligible base mile you earn, whether the mile comes from a flight, a Citi/AAdvantage card purchase, or an eligible partner.

The four status tiers

American held its status and reward levels steady for 2026, the third year in a row, so the thresholds below are the ones to plan around. The qualifying year runs from March 1 through the end of February, and Loyalty Points reset to zero every March 1. Status is valid from the moment you earn it through March 31 of the following full year.

How Loyalty Points are earned

  • Flying. On eligible American-marketed flights, base miles are generally five per dollar of base fare plus carrier charges, and each base mile earns one Loyalty Point. Status bonus miles (40% at Gold up to 120% at Executive Platinum) are bonus miles and do not earn Loyalty Points.
  • Cards. Citi/AAdvantage cards earn base miles at one per dollar on everyday purchases, so they earn Loyalty Points at about one per dollar. Category bonuses (for example, more miles on American purchases) add miles but not extra Loyalty Points.
  • Partners. Partner activity that earns eligible base miles also earns Loyalty Points, and at 60,000 Loyalty Points you can register for a 25% bonus on select partner spend.
  • Basic Economy. Basic Economy tickets purchased after December 17, 2025 earn neither miles nor Loyalty Points. Since May 18, 2026 they are also excluded from complimentary and systemwide upgrades.

What each tier actually gets you

Checked bags climb from one at Gold to three at Platinum Pro and Executive Platinum. Complimentary domestic upgrades open sooner with each tier: 24 hours before departure for Gold, 48 for Platinum, 72 for Platinum Pro and 100 for Executive Platinum. Those upgrades apply on eligible American flights within North America, on award tickets as well as paid tickets, and for one companion. Executive Platinum and Platinum Pro members have upgrade priority over lower tiers. The status levels also unlock a menu of Loyalty Point Rewards, covered below.

Editorial take: American is the cheapest of the three to earn status through flying alone, in dollars: 200,000 Loyalty Points is about $40,000 of fares for the top tier, and the entry tier is about $8,000. But it is also the program where Loyalty Points flow most easily from cards, which is why the card route is the main event here.

The credit-card route to American status

The big difference between AAdvantage and its rivals is that card spend and flying are interchangeable currencies. Every Citi/AAdvantage card earns Loyalty Points at about one per dollar, and the premium card adds milestone bonuses on top.

  • Citi / AAdvantage Executive World Legend Mastercard ($695 annual fee for new cardmembers from August 23, 2026): 1 Loyalty Point per dollar, plus 10,000 bonus Loyalty Points each at 50,000, 90,000, 165,000 and 240,000 Loyalty Points in the same status year, up to 40,000 bonus points. The refreshed card includes Admirals Club membership.
  • Any other Citi/AAdvantage card: 1 Loyalty Point per dollar on purchases that earn base miles, with no milestone bonus. Annual fees vary by card.

How to read these numbers

On card spend alone, Gold takes about $40,000, Platinum about $65,000 with the Executive card (or $75,000 on any other card), Platinum Pro about $105,000 and Executive Platinum about $170,000. That is the cheapest spend-only path to a top tier among the three programs, mostly because the card earns Loyalty Points one-for-one and the Executive card tacks on up to 40,000 more.

Mixing flying and card spend is where it gets practical. A traveler who flies about $10,000 of American fares in the year earns roughly 50,000 Loyalty Points from flying, which also triggers the Executive card's first 10,000-point milestone bonus. That puts Gold already in hand, and Platinum is about $15,000 of card spend away.

Do not spend to chase status. A card that earns a Loyalty Point per dollar is a reason to put existing spending on the right card, not a reason to buy things you do not need. If your normal spend is better rewarded on a flexible-points card, run the numbers first.

Award redemption flexibility

American splits its awards in two worlds: dynamic prices on its own flights and fixed, region-based charts on oneworld partners. Knowing which world you are in is the whole strategy.

Awards on American flights: dynamic

American retired its fixed MileSAAver and AAnytime award levels in April 2023 and moved awards on American-operated flights to dynamic prices. The "starting at" numbers on aa.com are estimates, and actual prices vary with demand. As with United and Delta, judge each award by cents per mile against the cash fare.

Awards on partners: fixed charts

Awards on oneworld and other partners remain on region-based partner award charts, so premium-cabin prices on carriers such as British Airways, Cathay Pacific, Qantas, Qatar Airways, Japan Airlines, Iberia and Finnair stay far more predictable than prices on American metal. That predictability is the main reason people with premium-cabin goals look hard at AAdvantage. Do compare taxes and fees: some partners, notably British Airways, can add substantial carrier-imposed surcharges on awards.

Alaska Airlines and the oneworld network

AAdvantage miles can book flights on Alaska Airlines, including Alaska-operated Hawaiian flights from April 22, 2026, and itineraries combining American with oneworld partners. For West Coast travelers, that makes AAdvantage more useful than American's own network might suggest. In the opposite direction, AAdvantage status members can get First Class upgrades and complimentary Premium Class seats on eligible Alaska fares.

Transfers

According to recent guides, Citi ThankYou points are the main bank-points currency that transfers directly to AAdvantage (at 1:1 from select Citi cards), while Chase and American Express points do not transfer directly. This detail changes, so confirm with the card issuer before relying on it.

Search tools and rules

  • Search: aa.com's "Redeem miles" option shows awards on American and partners. For some partner space, the partner's own site or Alaska's can show availability that is not obvious on aa.com.
  • Upgrades with status: complimentary upgrades work on award tickets, including for a companion within North America.
  • Systemwide upgrades: these are a Loyalty Point Reward and can be used on American flights worldwide and on British Airways transatlantic flights, for one-way trips of up to three segments.
  • Change and cancel rules, saver-space access and family pooling: we have not verified American's current award change and cancellation fees, saver-space rules or household pooling for this guide, so check aa.com before relying on them.
Award flexibility score: 4 / 5 (editorial judgment). Predictable partner charts across oneworld, the Alaska link, status upgrades that work on awards and a strong Loyalty Point Rewards ladder. Dynamic prices on American metal and thin bank transfer options keep it from a 5.

City fit: who should favor American

American counts nine hubs: Charlotte, Chicago O'Hare, Dallas–Fort Worth, Los Angeles, Miami, New York (one hub across JFK and LaGuardia), Philadelphia, Phoenix and Washington National. If you live near one, status and upgrades are most useful because your home-airport flights are American's strongest.

Where American is the natural default

  • Dallas–Fort Worth, Charlotte, Miami, Philadelphia and Phoenix. Each of these is an American hub and, among the three programs in this section, the only hub of the three at that airport. American status gets the most use at home.
  • Washington, D.C. (Reagan National). DCA is American's hub, while Dulles is United's. If you live near and use DCA, American is the home program.
  • New York. American shares JFK and LaGuardia with Delta, and United has Newark. Pick by the airport you use, then by your card and lounge preferences.
  • Chicago and Los Angeles. Both are multi-hub cities. American has a hub at O'Hare alongside United, and a hub at LAX alongside United and Delta. Decide by program features rather than geography.

Redemption optionality by city

Living at a hub gives you more nonstop choices and more award seats to look at. West Coast travelers get a special extra: because AAdvantage miles book Alaska Airlines and status members receive upgrades on Alaska, the program can be more useful there than American's own hub map suggests.

Competitive realities: where American dominates (for example, its home hubs), you can find fewer competing options. Price-shop even if you are loyal. And in multi-hub metros, American's edge is its program features, not geography.

Who American is not for

  • People without an American hub nearby. Status is worth much less when you connect through other airlines' hubs on most trips.
  • Basic Economy buyers. Basic Economy tickets bought after December 17, 2025 earn nothing, and since May 18, 2026 they are excluded from upgrades.
  • People who want to transfer Chase or Amex points directly. Those currencies do not transfer to AAdvantage; Citi ThankYou is the main route.
  • Anyone who expects lounge access from status. Admirals Club membership is a Loyalty Point Reward option at 250,000 Loyalty Points (it takes two reward choices), and it comes with the Executive card's annual fee, not with status.
  • Travelers who want fixed prices on American's own flights. Those awards are dynamic.
  • People who prefer Star Alliance or SkyTeam. American is a oneworld carrier; if your routes are served better by Star Alliance or SkyTeam partners, United or Delta fit better.
