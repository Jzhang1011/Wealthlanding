Snapshot: what MileagePlus is and how you earn status in 2026

MileagePlus pays you in United miles for flying and for spending on United-branded cards, and it grades you into four Premier tiers: Silver, Gold, Platinum and 1K. Status is earned in a calendar year with two different yardsticks that you can mix: Premier Qualifying Points (PQP), which track dollars, and Premier Qualifying Flights (PQF), which track how often you fly.

The two ways to qualify

For every tier you can either hit a PQP total and a PQF count, or skip the flight count by earning a larger PQP total on its own. United kept all of these thresholds unchanged for 2026 after raising them about 25% for 2025, so the numbers below are the ones to plan around. Two rules sit on top of both paths: you need to fly at least four segments on United or United Express in the year, and your qualifying window is the calendar year, January 1 through December 31.

How PQP and PQF are actually earned

  • PQP from flying. United counts roughly one PQP per dollar on the base fare and carrier-imposed charges of eligible United-operated tickets; government taxes are left out. That is why the table above shows a dollar equivalent beside each tier.
  • PQF from flying. You earn one PQF per flight segment on United, United Express and eligible partner airlines, so a one-stop trip counts as two. Basic Economy tickets do not earn PQF, and partner-operated award flights do not earn PQF either.
  • PQP from cards. Primary cardholders of eligible Chase United cards earn PQP on purchases, up to a per-card annual cap. That is the card route covered below.
  • Head start. Members who held Premier status as of February 1, 2026 received a PQP head start: 300 for Silver, 600 for Gold, 900 for Platinum and 1,400 for 1K. Expect United to say what, if anything, applies to 2027 in its usual late-year announcements.

What each tier actually gets you

The headline perks are bags and seats. Silver gets one free checked bag and Economy Plus seat selection at check-in; Gold and above get two bags (three at Platinum and 1K), Economy Plus at booking, and Star Alliance Gold treatment, which includes lounge access on eligible international itineraries but not on domestic ones. Complimentary Premier upgrades are available across the tiers on eligible flights, and since February 1, 2026 they also apply to tickets booked with miles. At Platinum and 1K, you receive PlusPoints (40 and 280 respectively), a flexible currency for buying upgrades or exchanging for other benefits.

Editorial take: Silver is mostly a baggage and seat perk. The jump in everyday value comes at Gold (second bag, Economy Plus at booking, international lounge access) and again at Platinum (more companions covered, PlusPoints). Whether a tier is worth chasing depends on how many segments you fly on United each year, not on the badge.

The credit-card route to United status

United is unusually friendly to card-based status. Eligible Chase United cards earn PQP on every purchase, and card PQP count toward every Premier tier, including the PQP-only requirement for 1K. The rate and the annual cap depend on the card:

  • United Club Card ($695 annual fee): 1 PQP per $15, up to 28,000 PQP a year, plus a 1,500-PQP annual jumpstart beginning the calendar year after you open it. It also includes United Club membership.
  • United Quest Card ($350 annual fee): 1 PQP per $20, up to 18,000 PQP a year, plus 1,000 bonus PQP each year (the card must have been open on December 31 of the prior year).
  • United Explorer and other entry cards: 1 PQP per $20 but with a low cap (1,000 PQP a year on the Explorer for 2026), so they help at the margins and do not scale into status.

How to read these numbers

Card-only status is a stunt, not a plan. Getting to Premier Gold on the Club Card alone takes about $157,500 of annual card spend. The practical use of the card route is to fill the gap: if you already fly United enough to earn 9,000 PQP from tickets, the Club Card closes the remaining 3,000 PQP to Gold with about $22,500 of spend (after the 1,500-PQP jumpstart). The Quest Card would need about $40,000 for the same gap.

Two caveats. First, spend that you would not otherwise make is never worth the miles: a card that earns one PQP per $15 is a poor reason to spend $15,000 you did not need to spend. Second, 1K is reachable on spend alone only with the Club Card (Quest tops out at 18,000 PQP from spend, which stops at Platinum), and United still requires the four flown segments.

Award redemption flexibility

The short version: United prices its own flights dynamically, offers good access to the Star Alliance, and removes most of the penalties that used to make awards stressful. Here is how that breaks down.

Dynamic pricing, with a Saver floor

United moved away from a published award chart several years ago. Awards on United-operated flights now come in two broad flavors: Saver awards, the lowest-priced and capacity-controlled tier, and higher-priced Everyday awards that appear when Saver space is gone. Prices move with the route and date, so there is no chart to memorize. The practical rule: search flexible dates, compare Saver against Everyday, and judge value as cents per mile against the cash fare, not against a chart.

Cardholder discounts and exclusive Saver space

Since early 2026, United gives primary United cardholders at least 10% off United-operated awards, and at least 15% off if they also hold Premier status. United also reserves extra Saver inventory, including in Polaris business class, for cardholders and Premier members; the airline says roughly a third of flights that show Saver fares have inventory exclusive to those groups. United's own examples: a 15,000-mile economy award becomes 13,500 for a cardholder, and a 200,000-mile Polaris seat becomes 170,000 for a Premier cardholder.

Star Alliance partner awards

MileagePlus miles also book Star Alliance partners, from Air Canada and Lufthansa Group to ANA, Singapore Airlines, Air New Zealand and EVA. Partner awards are where long-haul premium cabins can be most attractive per mile, but availability and pricing vary by airline and route, and some partners add their own surcharges. Always compare the miles plus the cash taxes and fees before you ticket. Lower-confidence area: United's partner pricing details change, so treat any specific partner price you read in a blog as a starting point and confirm it on united.com.

Transfers, pooling and fees

  • Chase Ultimate Rewards transfer to MileagePlus at 1:1. That lets you hold flexible Chase points until you find award space, then convert.
  • No change fees on United award tickets, miles never expire, and you can pool miles with family in a joint account.
  • Taxes and carrier charges still apply on every award, and some partner airlines add surcharges. Compare total cost.
  • Search tools: united.com and the United app show Saver and Everyday awards, the cardholder discount banner and partner space in one search. Multi-city searches help you price open-jaw ideas.

Upgrades on award tickets

From February 1, 2026 every Premier member is eligible for complimentary upgrades and PlusPoints upgrades on award tickets. That makes award travel with status more valuable than it used to be.

What to watch in 2027

United has said its fixed PlusPoints upgrade chart ends in February 2027 and PlusPoints prices will become demand-based. If you are holding a large PlusPoints balance, plan to use it before then, or be ready to see prices rise on popular flights.

Award flexibility score: 4 / 5 (editorial judgment). Strong partner network, no change fees, 1:1 Chase transfers and cardholder discounts. Dynamic pricing on United metal keeps it from a 5.

City fit: who should favor United

United lists seven U.S. hubs: Newark (for New York), Chicago O'Hare, Denver, Houston Bush Intercontinental, San Francisco, Los Angeles and Washington Dulles. If you live near one, United status is more useful because more of your flights leave from an airport where you hold priority, and awards are easier to find because hub flights are more numerous and more frequent.

Where United is the natural default

  • Newark and North Jersey. United's New York hub is Newark. If Newark is your airport, United is the obvious fit. If you live closer to JFK or LaGuardia, Delta and American are stronger there.
  • Houston, Denver and the Bay Area. These are single-hub situations for United among the three programs in this section, so United status gets the most use at home.
  • Chicago and Washington. You have a choice. At O'Hare, United and American both run hubs; at Dulles United is the hub while American's D.C. hub is at Reagan National. Choose by airport convenience and by which card ecosystem (Chase for United, Citi for American) fits you.
  • Los Angeles. United, American and Delta all list LAX as a hub, so no program is the default. United makes sense if you want Star Alliance partners for Asia and the Pacific and you value Chase transfers.

Redemption optionality by city

Living at a hub does two things for awards: you have more nonstop flights to choose from, and you often have more saver-level seats because there are more flights per day. A hub in a big metro also tends to be a Star Alliance gateway, which helps with partner awards to Europe and Asia. Smaller airports that connect through a United hub still work; they just add a segment.

Competitive realities: United's hub strength cuts both ways. In a market where United dominates, you may have fewer competitive options when fares matter most, so price-shop even as a loyalist. And in cities with several hubs (New York, Chicago, Los Angeles, Washington) status with one airline does not stop you from using another when the schedule or the fare is better.

Who United is not for

  • People without a United hub or strong United service near them. Status is worth much less if you connect through someone else's hub most of the time.
  • Travelers who want fixed award prices. United's own awards are dynamic and can swing. If you value a chart, partner awards on other programs may suit you better.
  • Anyone who expects domestic lounge access from status. Premier lounge access applies to international itineraries; domestic lounge access usually comes from a paid membership or a card such as the United Club Card.
  • Card-only status seekers. You still need to fly four United or United Express segments, and the spend required at higher tiers is large.
  • Basic Economy buyers. Basic Economy tickets do not earn PQF, so a year of cheapest-fare flying builds status more slowly than the PQP total suggests. Check the fare rules before you buy.
