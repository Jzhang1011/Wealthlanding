Snapshot: what SkyMiles is and how you earn status in 2026

SkyMiles is Delta's program and Medallion is its status ladder: Silver, Gold, Platinum and Diamond. Status is earned only with Medallion Qualification Dollars (MQDs). There is no separate miles or segments count to track. Delta's own qualification page lists MQD thresholds and nothing else, which makes the system easy to understand and a little hard on cheap fares.

The four Medallion tiers

Delta confirmed that MQD thresholds for 2027 status stay the same as 2026, with no added requirements. Status you earn from January 1 to December 31, 2026 is valid until January 31, 2028, and MQDs reset each calendar year.

How MQDs are earned

  • Delta and partner tickets. You earn MQDs based on the ticket price, excluding taxes, fees and Main Basic tickets, when you fly on Delta-ticketed flights or with more than 20 partner airlines. In plain terms: about $1 MQD per $1 of eligible fare.
  • Award tickets and mileage upgrades. Delta says award tickets and upgrades with miles (excluding Main Basic) earn $1 MQD per 100 miles redeemed.
  • Delta Amex cards. The Delta SkyMiles Platinum, Platinum Business, Reserve and Reserve Business cards give a $2,500 MQD Headstart per card type each year, and MQD Boost earns $1 MQD per $20 of eligible spend (Platinum cards) or per $10 (Reserve cards).
  • Choice Benefits and Delta Vacations. Platinum and Diamond members can pick an MQD Accelerator toward the next year, and eligible Delta Vacations bookings can earn MQDs under their own terms.

We did not find a card-spend MQD "waiver" on Delta's current qualification page; older guides mention one, so ignore any article that still treats a spend waiver as current.

What each tier actually gets you

Every Medallion tier gets unlimited complimentary upgrades for yourself and a companion (to Comfort+, First, and in the United States on domestic Delta One and Premium Select where those cabins exist), with the window widening by tier: Silver clears 24 hours before departure, Gold 72, and Platinum and Diamond 120 hours (with priority for Diamond). Comfort+ upgrades clear shortly after ticketing for Platinum and Diamond. Checked bags climb from one at Silver to three at Platinum and Diamond. Platinum members choose one Choice Benefit and Diamond members three, such as Regional or Global Upgrade Certificates, which outrank complimentary upgrades. Priority boarding, security lanes (at select airports) and SkyTeam Elite status come on top.

Editorial take: Delta status is the most valuable of the three at the top, thanks to Diamond's Choice Benefits, and the least forgiving at the bottom, because the MQD requirement is pure dollars and there is no flight-count shortcut. Silver and Gold are reachable with a Delta card's headstart plus modest flying; Diamond really is a flying-heavy goal.

The credit-card route to Delta status

Delta's card route is unusual: the cards deliver a free head start for simply holding them, and then add status dollars on spending.

  • Delta SkyMiles Reserve Amex ($650 annual fee): $2,500 MQD Headstart each year, plus 1 MQD per $10 of eligible spend. It includes 15 Sky Club visits a year, and $75,000 of eligible spend in a calendar year unlocks unlimited Sky Club access.
  • Delta SkyMiles Platinum Amex ($350 annual fee): the same $2,500 MQD Headstart but only 1 MQD per $20 of spend.
  • Business versions of both cards offer the same MQD headstart and boost rates.

Delta words the headstart as "per card type", so one card gets you halfway to Silver (which needs $5,000). Several guides read that wording as stacking across a Platinum-tier card and a Reserve-tier card; confirm the current terms with Delta or American Express before you rely on it.

How to read these numbers

On card spend alone, the Reserve gets you to Silver at $25,000, Gold at $75,000, Platinum at $125,000 and Diamond at $255,000. The Platinum card needs twice the spend at each level. Spend-only Diamond is a very large number and is mostly a curiosity.

The headstart makes the combination approach the practical one. A Reserve cardholder starts the year with 2,500 MQDs, so about $7,500 of Delta fares reaches Gold, with no extra card spend required. A traveler flying $5,000 of Delta fares in the year would need about $25,000 of Reserve spend to cover the rest of Gold and about $75,000 for Platinum.

Fees matter. A $650 card whose headstart covers half of Silver is only worth it if the lounge access, credits and flying you would do anyway justify the fee. Spending to chase MQDs rarely pays on its own.

Award redemption flexibility

Delta's awards are priced dynamically with no published award chart, but the rules around them are among the friendlier in the industry.

Pricing and partners

SkyMiles award prices vary by flight and date and are subject to capacity controls, so judge each award by cents per mile against the cash fare. Delta says there are no blackout dates on Delta-operated flights. You can also book awards on partner airlines through delta.com, including Air France, KLM, Korean Air, Virgin Atlantic, Aeromexico, LATAM, SAS and WestJet. Partner awards may price higher, may have blackout dates (Korean Air publishes theirs) and are not always available online. Delta's list of partners bookable on the site is shorter than the broad Star Alliance and oneworld networks, which is the main reason Delta scores lower here.

Changes, cancellations and fees

For awards that originate in the United States or Canada, you can change or cancel without a redeposit or change fee (except Delta Basic fares), though any mileage difference and taxes apply. Award taxes and fees are your responsibility: Delta notes a minimum of $11.20 in U.S. taxes on domestic itineraries and up to $600 in foreign taxes and carrier charges on international ones.

Booking for others, Miles + Cash and Pay with Miles

  • Book for anyone. You can book awards for yourself or someone else, even if you are not flying with them.
  • Miles + Cash. On select Delta flights you can pay for part of an award with money. These are award tickets with award rules, and they do not earn miles.
  • Pay with Miles (card benefit). Delta Amex cardmembers can use miles at up to $50 off per 5,000 miles on Delta tickets. That is a fixed value of about one cent per mile, which makes it a floor rather than a sweet spot.
  • Cardmember discount. Eligible Delta Amex cardmembers save 15% on Delta-operated award travel (not partners, taxes or fees).

Transfers

American Express Membership Rewards transfers to SkyMiles at 1:1, with a 1,000-point minimum. That is how Amex travel points reach Delta award space.

Award flexibility score: 2 / 5 (editorial judgment). Free changes, no blackouts on Delta flights and Amex transfers are real strengths. Dynamic prices with no chart, a narrower partner list and no family pooling that we could verify hold it back.

City fit: who should favor Delta

Delta operates hubs at Atlanta, Detroit, Minneapolis–St. Paul, Salt Lake City, Seattle, New York (JFK and LaGuardia), Boston and Los Angeles. Atlanta is its largest and most dominant hub. If you live near one, status and upgrades are most useful because your home-airport flights are Delta's strongest.

Where Delta is the natural default

  • Atlanta, Detroit, Minneapolis–St. Paul and Salt Lake City. These are Delta hubs where none of United or American runs a hub, so Delta is the practical home program for residents who want hub-based benefits.
  • Seattle and Boston. Delta has hubs at both. At Seattle, Alaska Airlines is also a major carrier, and we will cover that program soon. In Boston, other airlines compete heavily, so compare fares per trip.
  • New York. Delta shares JFK and LaGuardia with American, and United has Newark. Pick by airport and card preference.
  • Los Angeles. All three programs list LAX as a hub. Delta fits if you value Delta One and Delta's upgrade rules on its network, or if you plan to build status with an Amex card.

Redemption optionality by city

Hub residents see more nonstop options, and Delta's friendlier award change rules make it easier to book early. SkyTeam partners (Air France, KLM, Korean Air, Virgin Atlantic and others you can book on delta.com) add international reach, with the caveats about price and availability above.

Competitive realities: Delta's dominance at Atlanta, Detroit, Minneapolis and Salt Lake City cuts both ways: your status is most useful where choices are fewest. Price-shop, and in multi-hub cities do not treat loyalty to one airline as a rule.

Who Delta is not for

  • People who want cheap, predictable awards. Delta's awards are dynamic with no chart, and its partner list is narrower than the global alliances.
  • Cheapest-fare flyers. Because status is dollars only and Main Basic tickets earn no MQDs, buying the cheapest fares makes Medallion slow to earn.
  • People without a Delta hub nearby. Status is worth less when you connect through other airlines' hubs most of the time.
  • Anyone who wants Chase points to go straight to the airline. Amex Membership Rewards is the direct bank-points route to SkyMiles.
  • Card-only status seekers at the top. Diamond on spend alone takes about $255,000 of Reserve spend.
  • People who want a family pool. We could not verify a SkyMiles household pooling option.
