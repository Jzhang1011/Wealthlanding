Three programs, three different bets

United, American and Delta are the three largest U.S. network airlines, and each builds its loyalty program around a different idea. United rewards both dollars and flights and leans on a big hub network and Chase points. American counts Loyalty Points that flow almost one-for-one from card spending and fixes partner award prices across oneworld. Delta counts only dollars, but gives its cardholders a head start and keeps award rules friendly.

None of them is "the best" in the abstract. The right choice depends on three things you can check quickly: where you live, how you earn (flying versus card spend), and what you want status to do for you (upgrades, bags, lounge access or award discounts). The tools below help you answer each.

Which program for your city?

Hub presence matters more than most loyalty advice admits. If an airline runs a hub where you live, you get more nonstop options, more award seats to look at and more chances to use upgrades you earn. Choose your home metro to see which of the three has a hub there and why it may or may not fit.

How the picker decides: it starts from each airline's published U.S. hub list. A program is marked "Best fit" or "Strong fit" when it runs a hub at that metro's airport. When two or three hubs overlap (New York, Chicago, Los Angeles, Washington) the tool tells you to decide by airport convenience and by program features instead of picking a winner. It never claims route counts, and it does not rank smaller airports because nonstop reach there changes seasonally.

What the comparison tells you

  • Cheapest spend-only top tier: American. With one Loyalty Point per dollar and up to 40,000 bonus points on the Executive card, card spend alone reaches the top tier at a fraction of what United's 28,000 PQP asks.
  • Biggest card head start: Delta. A Platinum or Reserve card hands you $2,500 MQDs for holding it, so the first tier is half done before you spend a dollar. Delta is also the least forgiving program if you fly cheap fares.
  • Only card that reaches 1K on spend: United Club. The Club Card's higher PQP cap is the one route to United's top tier without a flying-heavy year, though four flown segments are still required.
  • Award flexibility: United and American score highest here for their partner networks. Delta's friendly rules (free changes, no blackouts on Delta flights) help, but dynamic prices with no chart and a shorter partner list hold it back.
  • City still decides. A great program at an airport where the airline has no hub is usually worse than an average program at your home hub.

Read the full guide for each program to see its hidden gems, the cards that fit, and who it is not for:

  • United MileagePlus guide: PQP and PQF, the Club and Quest cards, cardholder award discounts, hubs in Newark, Chicago, Houston, Denver, San Francisco, Los Angeles and Dulles.
  • American AAdvantage guide: Loyalty Points, the Executive card's bonuses, oneworld partner awards, hubs from Dallas and Charlotte to Miami, Philadelphia, Phoenix, New York and Washington National.
  • Delta SkyMiles guide: MQDs, the MQD Headstart, award rules, and hubs in Atlanta, Detroit, Minneapolis, Salt Lake City, Seattle, Boston, New York and Los Angeles.
