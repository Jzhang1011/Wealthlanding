Is airline status worth what it costs you?

Status pays you in free bags, extra-legroom seats, lounge visits and upgrades. It costs you the cash back you give up by putting spend on an airline card instead of a top cash-back card, plus the cards’ annual fees. Enter your travel, and the calculator does both sides in dollars.

Last verified October 15, 2026 Part of the airline loyalty hub

How the math works

Value side. Every benefit is counted only where the tier actually covers it: free bags up to the tier’s allowance (domestic and long-haul international allowances differ on United), for you plus the companions the benefit covers — United free bags cover you + up to 9 others on the same itinerary; Economy Plus covers +1 companion at Silver/Gold and +8 at Platinum/1K; preferred seating covers +8 at every tier; lounge visits count on international trips only, holder + 1 guest. On American, Main Cabin Extra covers +8 companions at every tier (Gold within 24 hours of departure); companion bag coverage from status alone is unverified, so only your own bags are counted; lounge visits count on qualifying international itineraries, +1 guest. On Delta, free bags cover you + up to 8 companions on the same Delta-operated reservation (except flights departing Paris CDG / Amsterdam AMS); Comfort+ covers +1 companion (Delta caps Comfort+ upgrades at one companion per reservation); and Delta status alone grants no Sky Club access on any itinerary, so lounge value from status is $0 — lounge access comes from cabin or a Choice Benefit membership, not status.

Cost side. The spend shown is the exact amount needed to earn the selected tier on the selected card alone — after yearly bonuses, headstarts, and milestone bonuses. The money you put on airline cards to earn status would have earned cash back on a top cash-back card. The calculator charges that gap — default 3% cash back vs 1% miles value, a 2% spread — on the required spend, then adds the cards’ annual fees. A card’s cap can make a tier unreachable on that card alone (e.g. United 1K on the Quest card); the calculator says so instead of inventing a number. Fares buy the travel itself, so flying is not charged as a cost; only the spend you route through airline cards is.

Gifting. Status you can give away is real value too. United lets you exchange PlusPoints for gifted status (Silver 40, Gold 100, Platinum 440, 1K 600 PlusPoints). American lets you pick gifted status as a Loyalty Point Reward (2 Gold gifts at 175,000 LP, Platinum gifts at 400,000+). Delta lets Platinum and Diamond members pick gifted status as a Choice Benefit (Silver for Platinum, Gold for Diamond). Enter your own estimate for what the gifted status is worth to the recipient; 0 leaves it out.

Cards and status. No United, American, or Delta card grants status outright — cards earn status currency (PQP, Loyalty Points, MQDs) through spend, which this calculator already counts. Delta cards add a $2,500 MQD headstart each year, no spend required, which the card-alone hint subtracts. (Some foreign carriers’ cards do grant status automatically, e.g. the Chase Aeroplan card’s yearly 25K status — but that’s outside these three programs.)

What is not counted. Preferred-seat selection, priority boarding, same-day change fee waivers, and award-ticket value are real but small or hard to price, so they are left out — the result is conservative. Complimentary upgrades (United’s CPU window, American’s upgrade list) are not given a dollar value either; PlusPoints and systemwide upgrades are, at your own valuation.

Your numbers beat our defaults. Bag fees, seat values and lounge values vary by route and season. Every price in the calculator is editable — change them to match your trips and the answer follows.
