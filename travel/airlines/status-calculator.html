Is airline status worth what it costs you?

Your airline card already covers free bags and lounge visits — with fewer restrictions than status. So this calculator only credits status with what it adds on top of your card: extra-legroom seats, upgrades, bonus miles, and bags or lounge visits beyond the card’s allowance. It costs you the cash back you give up by putting spend on an airline card instead of a top cash-back card. Enter your travel, and the calculator does both sides in dollars.

How the math works

The decision flow. (1) Your travel — how many round trips, with how many companions. (2) Your needs — which benefits you actually use, and what each is worth to you. (3) What your card already covers — a premium card gives you free bags and lounge access with no status qualification, no upgrade windows, no capacity limits. (4) What status adds — only the increment over the card. (5) What status costs — the cash back you give up on the spend needed to earn it. If the increment beats the cost, chasing status pays; if not, just hold the card.

Value side — status only counts what it adds. Free bags: the calculator takes the union of the status allowance and the card allowance per traveller, then subtracts what the card alone covers. A United Quest Card already gives you + 1 companion two free bags, so Gold status adds $0 in bags for that pair; an American card’s first-bag benefit is domestic-only, so status still adds your international bags. Lounge: same union-minus-card logic — a Club/Executive membership or Reserve’s 15 visits are the baseline, status adds only visits and guests beyond that. Extra-legroom seats, upgrades (PlusPoints, systemwide upgrades, Regional/Global certificates) and bonus miles are status-only — no card gives complimentary cabin upgrades or extra-legroom selection — so they count in full. On Delta, status alone grants no Sky Club access on any itinerary, so lounge value from status is $0 — lounge access comes from the card or cabin, not status.

Cost side. The spend shown is the exact amount needed to earn the selected tier on the selected card alone — after yearly bonuses, headstarts, and milestone bonuses. The money you put on airline cards to earn status would have earned cash back on a top cash-back card. The calculator charges that gap — default 2% cash back vs 1% miles value, a 1% spread — on the required spend. Card fees are not counted by default, because you pay them whether or not you chase status (untick “I already hold this card” to count them). A card’s cap can make a tier unreachable on that card alone (e.g. United 1K on the Quest card); the calculator says so instead of inventing a number. Fares buy the travel itself, so flying is not charged as a cost; only the spend you route through airline cards is.

Gifting. Status you can give away is real value too. United lets you exchange PlusPoints for gifted status (Silver 40, Gold 100, Platinum 440, 1K 600 PlusPoints). American lets you pick gifted status as a Loyalty Point Reward (2 Gold gifts at 175,000 LP, Platinum gifts at 400,000+). Delta lets Platinum and Diamond members pick gifted status as a Choice Benefit (Silver for Platinum, Gold for Diamond). Enter your own estimate for what the gifted status is worth to the recipient; 0 leaves it out.

Cards and status. No United, American, or Delta card grants status outright — cards earn status currency (PQP, Loyalty Points, MQDs) through spend, which this calculator already counts. But cards do hand you status-like perks with no qualification: free checked bags (United: holder + 1 companion, 1 bag on Explorer, 2 on Quest/Club; American: holder + up to 8 companions on Executive/Globe, + 4 on other cards, domestic itineraries only; Delta: holder + up to 8 companions on Gold/Platinum/Reserve) and lounge access (United Club membership on the Club card; Admirals Club membership on Executive; 4 passes a year on Globe; 15 Sky Club visits a year on Reserve). Those are the baseline this calculator subtracts. Delta cards add a $2,500 MQD headstart each year, no spend required, which the card-alone hint subtracts. (Some foreign carriers’ cards do grant status automatically, e.g. the Chase Aeroplan card’s yearly 25K status — but that’s outside these three programs.)

What is not counted. Preferred-seat selection, priority boarding, same-day change fee waivers, and award-ticket value are real but small or hard to price, so they are left out — the result is conservative. Complimentary upgrades (United’s CPU window, American’s upgrade list) are not given a dollar value either; PlusPoints and systemwide upgrades are, at your own valuation.

Your numbers beat our defaults. Bag fees, seat values and lounge values vary by route and season. Every price in the calculator is editable — change them to match your trips and the answer follows.
